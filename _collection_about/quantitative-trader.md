I made my first trade in April 2004 and have traded FX, options, futures, commodities and
crypto since. That gives me a working sense of market mechanics: which ideas are worth
researching and which waste time. I build the production systems I trade on, from
backtesting engines to order management and direct market access.

## Approach
- **Where, how, when.** Fundamental factors set the market's direction of gravity (where). Quantified sentiment models test the hypothesis (how). Price action times the entry (when).
- **Three alpha types.** My portfolio combines directional strategies, options (volatility) strategies, and relative value: pairs, spreads and arbitrage.
- **Market neutrality.** I offset each directional position with an opposite position in another asset. My book should not read as net long or net short.
- **Volatility-normalised sizing.** Position size scales to current volatility against a target risk level. A position carries about the same risk in 2008 as in 2013, and in Bitcoin as in EURUSD.
- **Monte-Carlo backtesting.** A historical price path is one outcome among many. I stress-test systems against simulated price paths to find weak points a single backtest hides.

## Circle of competence
- **Systematic directional trading.** Small universes of major asset classes rather than single stocks: stock indexes, FX, commodity futures and bond futures.
- **Options.** Trading and researching options since 2008, on my own pricing models. As a net options seller, I control risk with volatility-normalised sizing and Monte-Carlo simulation.
- **Pairs and relative value.** Keeps the portfolio market-neutral, diversifies against large market moves and smooths returns.
- **Fair-value models.** Price is what you pay, value is what you get. Even in short-term trading, I use fair value as the baseline that shows which way slow forces push price.
